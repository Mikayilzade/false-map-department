## 1. Research rules

1. Start from current evidence, not from Organism Cargo's shortlist.
2. Extract durable player desires and interaction patterns rather than cloning current hits.
3. Prefer hooks understandable in one sentence and demonstrable in a short clip.
4. Prefer reusable systemic depth over large bespoke content libraries.
5. Treat multiplayer as a high-cost exception, not a default.
6. Rapidly cloning microgenres are warnings unless the interaction model changes substantially.
7. Weird specificity is useful only when the underlying action remains satisfying after the joke wears off.
8. Every serious candidate needs a one-week graybox that can prove or kill its core.
9. A candidate that survives on theme but not on verbs is rejected.
10. Avoid converging automatically on the plan/commit/watch structure used by Game #001 unless the new game earns it independently.

## 2. Current market / discovery signals — August 2026

### 2.1 Discovery pressure is severe
GameDiscoverCo's June 2026 Next Fest analysis tracked 4,382 demos, roughly 66% more than June 2025, while follower gains for top-percentile games were lower year over year. GameSpot independently described more than 4,000 demos.

Sources:
- https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-june-2026s-steam-next-fest
- https://www.gamespot.com/articles/steam-next-fest-june-2026-25-of-the-best-demos-you-can-play-right-now/

Implication: merely being polished or joining a festival is not discovery. The game needs a legible hook in thumbnail/trailer/demo form.

### 2.2 Oversupply is a design constraint
A 2026 Steam metadata study describes extreme concentration of player attention and increasing marketplace oversupply. Exact causal claims are research hypotheses, but the practical discovery problem is consistent with current festival volume.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25010

Implication: low production cost alone is not an advantage; distinctiveness and retention must be designed.

### 2.3 Weird specificity can cut through
PC Gamer highlighted *Horse Magnifier: The Full Horse* in August 2026: an absurdly specific noun+verb premise supported by real puzzle structure rather than novelty alone.

Source:
- https://www.pcgamer.com/games/puzzle/this-weeks-most-compelling-new-steam-game-is-horse-magnifier-which-is-about-magnifying-horses/

Implication: preserve memorable names and actions instead of sanding concepts into generic genre descriptions.

### 2.4 Viral small-team games reward instantly legible stories, but multiplayer is dangerous scope
Coverage of 2026 breakout *Meccha Chameleon* describes a two-person low-price multiplayer game whose camouflage interactions generated highly shareable clips. The reported sales numbers are press-reported rather than audited.

Source:
- https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/the-viral-hit-of-2026-has-sold-15-million-copies-in-a-month-on-steam-costs-usd5-and-was-made-by-2-people

Implication: borrow clipability and player-generated stories, not necessarily networking.

### 2.5 Generic task simulators are visibly crowded
PC Gamer documented a flood of retail simulators and a rapid wave of shelf-sorting/tidying games in July–August 2026.

Sources:
- https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-torrent-of-janky-retail-sims-continues-to-flood-steam-and-theres-no-end-in-sight/
- https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-games-about-sorting-1000s-of-mundane-objects-onto-shelves-is-the-new-craze-sweeping-pc-gaming/

Implication: "satisfying work" remains valid, but ordinary retail, sorting, cleaning and job-sim framing have a high burden of proof.

### 2.6 Compact programming remains viable but occupied
*Problem Space* demonstrates a current audience for command-sequence optimization, but also means terminal/programming presentation is not novel by itself.

Source:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4772820/Problem_Space_Demo/

### 2.7 One deep mechanic plus modular production remains a strong small-team pattern
The *Turing Test* postmortem explicitly describes building around one singular puzzle mechanic and modular environment production.

Source:
- https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-building-i-the-turing-test-i-around-a-secret-mechanic

### 2.8 Fast arcade identity still has room when the verb is distinctive
2026 coverage of *Denshattack!* shows a stylized arcade game can break through when movement itself is the fantasy and the demo communicates that quickly.

Source:
- https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/denshattack-dev-wakes-up-rolls-out-of-bed-finds-out-their-game-is-one-of-the-highest-rated-releases-of-2026-and-promptly-loses-their-mind/

Implication: Game #002 should not be biased toward slow puzzle/simulation simply because that is efficient to specify.

### 2.9 Desktop/background games are now a visible microtrend, not empty territory
Recent examples include *Desktop Heroes*, *Still Open: Taskbar Cafe Idle*, *Desktop Oasis*, *Desktop Nard*, *Widle* and *Desktop Pachinko Idle*.

Sources:

- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4688280/Desktop_Oasis/

## 4. Direct competitor / analogue checks for Run-1 Tier A

### G2C01 — Shadow Printer
Closest current analogue found: *Projected Dreams* (released 2025), where players rotate/place/stack toys specifically to recreate target shadow silhouettes.

Source:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2318800/Projected_Dreams/

Other shadow-adjacent games include *Projection: First Light* and *In The Shadows*.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/726490/Projection_First_Light/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/470580/In_The_Shadows/

Decision: **downgrade sharply**. The original "arrange objects to make a shadow" marketing image is no longer distinct enough. It could survive only if the shadow becomes a persistent executable system rather than a target silhouette; that is now a materially different concept and should not inherit the original score automatically.

### G2C19 — False Map Department
*Map Map — A Game About Maps* released May 2026 and has players make maps with navigation tools. *Bilkins' Folly* also uses player cartography for puzzles.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2702260/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1526370/Bilkins_Folly/

Key distinction: those examples use maps to represent/navigate a world. G2C19's hook is **ontological cartography**: edits to representation alter the represented world itself. Competitive overlap is thematic, not yet mechanical.

Decision: **retain strongly**, but the graybox must prove the world-change consequence is immediate and readable rather than feeling like drawing arbitrary puzzle answers.

### G2C20 — Creature Costume Inspector
Current inspection/deduction territory is active. *Security 51* asks players to scan staff and verify who may be non-human. *Yokai Landlord: Monster Mystery!* asks players to identify disguised yokai using clues/evidence.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4351470
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3193560/

Decision: **retain with caution**. The differentiator must be tactile layered disguise + limited physical tests that transform evidence, not another document/checkpoint deduction game. If play collapses to reading clues and picking a suspect, reject.

### G2C05 — Echo Sculptor
Audio-first puzzle games exist: *Echo Maze* uses 3D audio in darkness, and *Invisible Room* is an audio-based puzzle/investigation game.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3927440/Echo_Maze/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3533210/Invisible_Room/

No close result in this pass matched the exact mechanic of editing reflective geometry/materials to sculpt a propagating sound field.

Decision: **retain**, but accessibility and implementation are first-class kill risks. It cannot require expensive physically accurate acoustics; a discrete graph/ray model must still feel like sound.

### G2C09 — Weather Locksmith
Targeted Steam search found weather-themed games but no close current result matching compact pressure/temperature/wind/moisture state composition as the primary puzzle verb.

Decision: **retain**. Main risk is abstraction: sliders disguised as weather would be dead on arrival. The graybox must make weather behave spatially and visibly.

### G2C03 — Reverse Heist Lab
The security/thief space is established: *Thief Simulator*, *Intruder*, *Supermarket Guard Simulator* and many camera/security games already use surveillance, traps or intrusion.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/704850/Thief_Simulator/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/518150/Intruder/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4294250/Supermarket_Guard_Simulator_Demo/

The deterministic "design a defense, run an explainable adversary, diagnose the exploit" loop is still less common, but it also resembles Game #001's plan/commit/observe/revise structure.

Decision: **reserve, not finalist by default**. It must beat both market familiarity and factory repetition.

### G2C06 — Fish Compiler
*Fish Game* proves an aquarium with dynamic behavior/ecology has an audience, while current programming puzzle games prove rule construction itself is occupied.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1372150/Fish_Game/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4772820/Problem_Space_Demo/

Decision: **retain** because physical rule tiles + visible fish behavior is a distinct presentation, but tutorial burden is severe. The first five minutes must work without pseudo-code literacy.

### G2C22 — The Last Stagehand
The theatre/backstage theme has more overlap than expected. *Staged* (Q3 2026) is a 1–4 player stage-rebuilding physics game; *Once Upon a Puppet* uses a stagehand and prop/backdrop arrangement; *Stagehand Survival Simulator* and *Impresario* also occupy backstage/production fantasies.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3534100/Staged/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1904880/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2094660/Stagehand_Survival_Simulator/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3713600/Impresario__Theater_Tycoon/

Decision: **downgrade**. The unattended cascading-performance mechanic remains interesting, but the marketing noun "stagehand" is no longer scarce enough to carry the concept.

## 6. Harsher Phase-1 elimination model

Run-1 weighted scores overvalued novelty/scope and underweighted feel. The gate is replaced by ten dimensions scored 1–5:

- **Hook (15%)** — sentence/screenshot/GIF clarity.
- **Core pleasure (15%)** — would the primary verb feel good before progression/content?
- **Systemic depth (12.5%)** — can a small rule vocabulary recombine?
- **Repetition resistance (10%)** — plausible hour-10 variation without massive authored volume.
- **Scope fitness (10%)** — small-team production/QA burden.
- **One-week graybox proof (10%)** — can the central claim be proven cheaply?
- **Demo strength (10%)** — can 5–15 minutes produce desire rather than only explanation?
- **Tutorial burden (5%)** — lower teaching cost scores higher.
- **Technical risk (7.5%)** — deterministic/reliable implementation feasible.
- **Competitive whitespace (5%)** — direct modern analogue penalty.

Hard kill conditions override averages:
1. core pleasure cannot be demonstrated in a one-week graybox;
2. hook depends mainly on theme after close competitor stripping;
3. requires uncontrolled physics/acoustics/AI to be fun;
4. first meaningful decision needs long textual onboarding;
5. hour-10 depth requires hundreds of bespoke puzzles/assets;
6. visually indistinguishable from a crowded microgenre in a muted 5-second clip;
7. accessibility would require removing the central mechanic rather than adapting presentation;
8. a stronger known game already delivers substantially the same noun + verb + fantasy.

## 7. Phase-1 finalist slate for the next tournament

This is the first slate that has survived direct overlap checks and field broadening. It is intentionally diverse; final selection is **not** made here.

### F1 — G2C19 False Map Department
**Why alive:** extraordinary sentence/GIF hook; representation literally rewrites reality; thematic map competition exists but close mechanic not found.
**Core risk:** arbitrary puzzle-author logic; drawing may become fiddly.
**One-week proof:** tiny 6-node world where adding/removing/connecting symbols immediately changes traversability, resources and inhabitants with reversible deterministic rules.

### F2 — G2C20 Creature Costume Inspector
**Why alive:** tactile deduction, strong character comedy/visual identity, compact room/content scope.
**Core risk:** inspection games are active; must not become documents + suspect selection.
**One-week proof:** three layered disguises, four physical tests, evidence changes depending on test order, one reveal animation.

### F3 — G2C05 Echo Sculptor
**Why alive:** uncommon primary verb, strong audiovisual feedback, systemic medium.
**Core risk:** acoustics complexity and audio accessibility.
**One-week proof:** 2D discrete sound rays/graph with reflect/absorb/resonate surfaces and visible accessibility overlay; verify that shaping a room is satisfying without realistic simulation.

### F4 — G2C09 Weather Locksmith
**Why alive:** no close primary-mechanic competitor found; weather offers visible interacting state variables and strong transformations.
**Core risk:** could degenerate into parameter sliders or chemistry homework.
**One-week proof:** one chamber, four spatial emitters, condensation/wind/pressure interactions, three devices unlocked by emergent local conditions rather than exact numeric targets.

### F5 — G2C06 Fish Compiler
**Why alive:** programs are embodied as creatures immediately showing consequences; systemic depth can come from a tiny vocabulary.
**Core risk:** programming tutorial burden; aquarium/ecosystem competition.
**One-week proof:** 5 rule tiles, 3 fish types, 3 goals; non-programmers should predict and revise behavior after one demonstration.

### F6 — G2C07 Impossible Elevator
**Why alive:** excellent scope, one-screen readability, potentially deep state-transition puzzle.
**Core risk:** weaker marketing identity; may become abstract scheduling.
**One-week proof:** 5 floors, 6 passenger traits, 3 rules that mutate on boarding/exiting; test whether surprising consequences are readable.

### F7 — G2C23 Pocket Weather War
**Why alive:** same weather vocabulary as F4 but shifts from lock puzzle to adversarial board/tactics; potentially much higher replayability.
**Core risk:** harder balancing and visualization; overlaps F4 enough that only one may survive tournament.
**One-week proof:** discrete 8×8 board, two climate seeds, terrain alters fronts, deterministic forecast preview, 5-minute matches versus script opponent.

### F8 — G2C28 Orbit Graffiti
**Why alive:** strongest non-puzzle addition; expressive movement can create trailer identity, score attack and mastery with low content burden if rails are systemic.
**Core risk:** controls/physics feel; gravity/orbit theme already familiar.
**One-week proof:** one planet cluster, draw one temporary rail, avatar auto-locks/skates, speed/trick multiplier, delivery target; determine whether drawing + riding forms one fluid verb.

### F9 — G2C30 One-Room Convoy
**Why alive:** tactical real-time pressure in a tiny reusable arena; content comes from unit/hazard combinations rather than maps.
**Core risk:** looks generic without a stronger fiction/visual grammar.
**One-week proof:** looping arena with 4 junctions, 3 autonomous units, 3 switch types, escalating hazards; player can only change two junctions at once.

### F10 — G2C34 Heat Signature
**Why alive:** indirect stealth where temperature rather than direct character control creates routes; tactile systemic cause/effect.
**Core risk:** can become another plan/watch simulator or invisible-state spreadsheet.
**One-week proof:** one compact room network with guards/sensors/doors that visibly react to thermal zones in real time; player must improvise rather than precompute entire solution.

## 8. Tournament questions for Run 3

Phase 2 must not begin as a beauty contest. Each finalist needs the same destructive packet:

1. What is the exact repeated 10-second verb?
2. What produces pleasure before progression/rewards?
3. What changes by minute 30, hour 3 and hour 10?
4. What is the likely dominant strategy?
5. What is the most boring valid way to play?
6. What does a 10-second trailer clip show with UI muted?
7. What must a one-week graybox prove, and what result kills the concept?
8. What close competitor would a Steam shopper compare it to first?
9. What content burden appears after 20 levels/runs?
10. What accessibility adaptation is required without deleting the core mechanic?
11. What implementation subsystem is most likely to explode scope?
12. Can the game create stories or mastery clips without multiplayer?
13. Is the premise still attractive when stripped of its title/theme?
14. Does the concept repeat any Game #001 structural dependency enough to reduce portfolio diversity?
