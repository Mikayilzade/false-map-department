## 1. Run-3 competitor checks

### F6 — Impossible Elevator
Current elevator territory is visibly occupied. `Lift Attendant` is already a compact passenger-routing/efficiency puzzle, while `MR. ELEVATOR`, `Separium: 12th Elevator`, `Elevator` and multiple anomaly/horror titles make the noun itself noisy.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3035940/Lift_Attendant/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2387470/MR_ELEVATOR/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3315300/Separium_12th_Elevator/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4203470/_Elevator/

Conclusion: the mutation-rule mechanic remains potentially good, but the first Steam comparison would be elevator scheduling or anomaly horror. Marketing identity is weaker than the other finalists.

### F7 — Pocket Weather War
Current tactics games frequently use weather as a modifier, including `Tide Of Tactics`, while 4X/strategy games such as `BOC: Birth of Cultures` model climate dynamically. No close result in this pass makes **creating and steering competing weather fronts** the primary tactical verb on a tiny board.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4005630/Tide_Of_Tactics/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1159150/BOC_Birth_of_Cultures/

Conclusion: weather-as-system is familiar; weather-as-army still has useful whitespace if the player directly seeds/redirects fronts rather than merely reacting to random weather.

### F8 — Orbit Graffiti
The competitive risk increased. `Stunted Gravity` (Q3 2026) explicitly sells gravity-shifting stunt lines, score attack and improvisational movement. `Stunt Xpress` combines deliveries with gravity-defying stunts. Older/current gravity games also normalize gravity manipulation.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4103290/Stunted_Gravity/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2645830/Stunt_Xpress/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/3955270/Gravity/

Conclusion: gravity stunts alone are not whitespace. Orbit Graffiti survives only because **drawing a temporary rail and then riding the exact line you drew** can become a single expressive verb visible in a clip.

### F9 — One-Room Convoy
`Convoy` already owns a strong vehicular tactical noun, and `GRIDROAD` plus `Road Builder` cover junction/traffic optimization. None of these matches a small looping combat/escort arena where multiple autonomous units are redirected under live pressure.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/318230/Convoy/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/2677310/GRIDROAD/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/1715000/Road_Builder/

Conclusion: mechanically defensible, but the word **Convoy** should not survive as a final title and the fiction must clearly separate the game from traffic optimization.

### F10 — Heat Signature
A major naming collision exists: `Heat Signature` (2017) is a highly rated, well-known top-down stealth/action game. Separately, `Sole Mistake` demonstrates current appetite for indirect control through doors/lights/sound while observing an agent remotely.

Sources:
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/268130/Heat_Signature/
- https://store.steampowered.com/app/4670090/Sole_Mistake/

Conclusion: the working title is unusable. More importantly, the indirect-control fantasy is less scarce than expected; temperature would need to transform moment-to-moment play enough to justify the extra simulation burden.

## 2. Uniform destructive packet — all ten finalists

Each answer is deliberately compressed to decision-relevant evidence.

### F1 — False Map Department
1. **10-second verb:** draw, erase or reconnect one map symbol/route and immediately inspect the rewritten tiny world.
2. **Pleasure before rewards:** visual cause/effect between representation and reality; discovering non-obvious consequences.
3. **Minute 30 / hour 3 / hour 10:** more symbol types -> interacting map conventions -> multi-layer maps where one edit helps one system and harms another.
4. **Dominant strategy risk:** brute-force every legal edit and undo.
5. **Most boring valid play:** exhaustive trial-and-error without forming a model.
6. **Muted trailer:** hand draws bridge/river/border; miniature world physically changes at once.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if players cannot predict at least the direction of effects after a short tutorial or if drawing precision dominates reasoning.
8. **First comparison:** `Map Map`, `Bilkins' Folly`, perhaps rule-changing puzzlers; exact representation-rewrites-reality hook remains uncommon.
9. **Content burden:** moderate; needs rule-combination scenarios, but symbolic systems can recombine instead of requiring bespoke art.
10. **Accessibility:** snap-to-node drawing, keyboard/controller symbol placement, high-contrast world/map linkage; freehand precision cannot be mandatory.
11. **Scope explosion:** state dependency graph and reversible world updates.
12. **Stories/mastery clips:** yes—one tiny line causing a town, river or creature route to transform is inherently clip-friendly.
13. **Theme stripped:** still attractive as a graph-editing system that mutates another simulation.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low-to-moderate. It is iterative and causal, but effects occur immediately during editing rather than plan/commit/watch.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — strongest hook/system balance.**

### F2 — Creature Costume Inspector
1. **10-second verb:** choose a physical test, apply it to one disguise layer, read transformed evidence.
2. **Pleasure:** tactile reveal, suspicion, comedy, deduction from changed evidence.
3. **Progression:** simple visual tells -> tests that destroy/alter evidence -> creatures that deliberately exploit test order.
4. **Dominant strategy:** always use the highest-information test first.
5. **Boring play:** checklist every test on every subject.
6. **Muted trailer:** squeeze/brush/heat/light a ridiculous costume; hidden anatomy reacts.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if optimal play becomes fixed test order or if deduction is mostly reading text.
8. **First comparison:** `Security 51`, `Yokai Landlord`, checkpoint/inspection games.
9. **Content burden:** medium-high because disguises/reactions need authored identity; can be contained with modular layered components.
10. **Accessibility:** every clue requires visual+text/icon redundancy; tests selectable without timing/fine motor demands.
11. **Scope explosion:** combinatorial authored reactions and dialogue/animation expectations.
12. **Stories/clips:** strong reveals and mistaken assumptions can produce shareable moments.
13. **Theme stripped:** weaker; the tactile creature fiction carries meaningful value.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low. Continuous evidence gathering, no committed simulation phase.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — commercially legible, but content-cost and fixed-test-order risks are serious.**

### F3 — Echo Sculptor
1. **10-second verb:** rotate/place/change one acoustic surface and immediately send a pulse through the room.
2. **Pleasure:** seeing/hearing a wave bend, split, cancel or resonate exactly because of your edit.
3. **Progression:** reflection -> absorption/resonance -> phase/timing/multiple sources -> moving or stateful receivers.
4. **Dominant strategy:** build obvious mirror corridors or spam absorbers.
5. **Boring play:** trial-rotate every panel until target lights.
6. **Muted trailer:** visible wavefront ricochets and lights a shape/path; accessibility overlay can sell the mechanic even muted.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if the simplified model feels arbitrary or realistic acoustics are required for intuition.
8. **First comparison:** audio puzzles such as `Echo Maze`/`Invisible Room`; geometry-sculpting acoustic field remains distinct.
9. **Content burden:** low-to-medium if rooms are modular and rule-driven.
10. **Accessibility:** full visual wave/energy representation, captions/haptics; audio cannot be the sole information channel.
11. **Scope explosion:** acoustic simulation and edge cases if too physically ambitious.
12. **Stories/clips:** beautiful wave cascades and elegant minimal solutions have mastery-clip potential.
13. **Theme stripped:** still strong as field-routing geometry puzzle.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low. Immediate manipulation/probing rather than commit/watch.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — high originality, but graybox feel is existential.**

### F4 — Weather Locksmith
1. **10-second verb:** place/retune a local weather source and watch nearby humidity/wind/temperature/pressure interact.
2. **Pleasure:** creating visible micro-weather and emergent transitions such as condensation, fog, gusts, icing.
3. **Progression:** single-variable states -> coupled states -> spatial feedback loops -> moving mechanisms.
4. **Dominant strategy:** memorize recipes or brute-force emitter combinations.
5. **Boring play:** slider tuning until an indicator turns green.
6. **Muted trailer:** tiny chamber visibly fogs/freezes/vents and machinery unlocks.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if spatial weather is less important than numeric targets.
8. **First comparison:** weather-themed puzzles/sims rather than a direct close match.
9. **Content burden:** moderate handcrafted chambers; systemic combinations can help.
10. **Accessibility:** strong icons/patterns for conditions; no color-only pressure/temperature encoding.
11. **Scope explosion:** fluid/field simulation if fidelity grows.
12. **Stories/clips:** moderate; pretty transformations, less spontaneous than adversarial weather.
13. **Theme stripped:** medium; underlying multi-variable state puzzle remains valid but less exciting.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low-to-moderate causal systems, but immediate interactive manipulation.

**Verdict: ELIMINATED IN WEATHER DUEL — F7 offers a more differentiated audience and stronger replay path from the same expensive vocabulary.**

### F5 — Fish Compiler
1. **10-second verb:** attach/reorder one behavior tile and immediately observe a fish execute it.
2. **Pleasure:** embodied program becomes visible animal behavior; debugging without code text.
3. **Progression:** simple triggers -> priorities -> inter-fish signals -> reusable behavior modules.
4. **Dominant strategy:** universal safe script/template.
5. **Boring play:** copy known scripts and wait.
6. **Muted trailer:** snap pictogram tiles together; fish instantly perform a clever chain.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if non-programmers need formal logic vocabulary or spend more time reading than watching fish.
8. **First comparison:** programming puzzlers plus `Fish Game`/aquarium sims.
9. **Content burden:** low art, high tutorial/balance burden.
10. **Accessibility:** icon+text redundant tiles, step-through mode, no timing requirement.
11. **Scope explosion:** priority semantics, debugging UI and pathological rule interactions.
12. **Stories/clips:** moderate; emergent fish failures can be funny but risk becoming opaque.
13. **Theme stripped:** behavior-programming remains mechanically sound but market niche narrows.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** moderate: configure -> release -> observe -> revise is structurally close.

**Verdict: ELIMINATED — strong system, but tutorial burden plus explicit configure/release/observe portfolio repetition loses to more portfolio-diverse concepts.**

### F6 — Impossible Elevator
1. **10-second verb:** choose next floor/boarding action and resolve passenger/floor rule mutation.
2. **Pleasure:** compact state puzzle with surprising rule interactions.
3. **Progression:** passenger traits -> floor mutations -> persistent route conditions -> chained obligations.
4. **Dominant strategy:** solve as scheduling optimization or search state tree.
5. **Boring play:** spreadsheet-like queue optimization.
6. **Muted trailer:** passengers enter/exit; floor labels/rules mutate, but the hook is less instantly legible than top contenders.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if surprising mutations read as arbitrary bookkeeping.
8. **First comparison:** `Lift Attendant` first, then crowded elevator/anomaly games.
9. **Content burden:** low-to-medium.
10. **Accessibility:** excellent one-screen potential; icons/text redundant.
11. **Scope explosion:** state-space validation and solvability testing.
12. **Stories/clips:** weaker; intellectual reveals more than expressive clips.
13. **Theme stripped:** becomes abstract scheduling/state puzzle.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low structural repetition, but both rely on deterministic state interaction.

**Verdict: ELIMINATED — efficient to build but weakest marketing identity among survivors.**

### F7 — Pocket Weather War
1. **10-second verb:** seed, heat/cool, redirect or terrain-shape a front while watching the forecast update.
2. **Pleasure:** visibly steering a living front into an opponent while countering theirs.
3. **Progression:** two-front basics -> interacting terrain/moisture -> asymmetric climate tools -> multi-objective maps and advanced forecast manipulation.
4. **Dominant strategy:** rush one extreme climate or turtle behind stable terrain.
5. **Boring play:** repeat a solved opening and wait for forecast resolution.
6. **Muted trailer:** tiny sunny/rain/snow fronts collide, curl around mountains and consume territory.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if the 8×8 forecast is unreadable, outcomes feel random, or optimal openings collapse variety.
8. **First comparison:** tactics games with weather modifiers, but not a close weather-is-army primary verb.
9. **Content burden:** low-to-medium if board seeds/objectives recombine.
10. **Accessibility:** icons/patterns/arrows plus forecast timeline; no color-only front ownership/state.
11. **Scope explosion:** deterministic front simulation plus AI/balance, especially if continuous fluid modeling is attempted.
12. **Stories/clips:** strong front collisions/reversals; asynchronous challenges could work without networking.
13. **Theme stripped:** still appealing as competing deterministic fields over terrain.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** low if turns are interactive and forecasts update continuously; avoid commit-and-watch rounds.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — wins weather duel; must remain a readable tactics game, not meteorology homework.**

### F8 — Orbit Graffiti
1. **10-second verb:** draw a curved gravity rail, snap onto it, ride/launch from it into the next stunt or delivery.
2. **Pleasure:** authorship of a line followed immediately by embodied execution of that line.
3. **Progression:** simple arcs -> rail linking -> momentum transfer -> hazards/deliveries/trick constraints and user-expression score routes.
4. **Dominant strategy:** draw one safe high-speed orbit and reuse it.
5. **Boring play:** overdraw slow safe rails, ignore tricks.
6. **Muted trailer:** player draws glowing curve around planet then surfs it at speed into another orbit.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if drawing interrupts flow, rails feel like path planning rather than movement, or safe lines dominate stylish ones.
8. **First comparison:** `Stunted Gravity` and `Stunt Xpress`; differentiation must be player-authored rails as movement itself.
9. **Content burden:** low-to-medium; planetary geometry/hazards can recombine.
10. **Accessibility:** aim assist, snap curves, time-slow while drawing, controller/keyboard rail primitives; precision freehand cannot be required.
11. **Scope explosion:** movement feel, collision/rail attachment physics, camera.
12. **Stories/clips:** excellent; high mastery and failure clips.
13. **Theme stripped:** still strong—draw a path, physically ride it, chain momentum.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** very low; action/skill/flow radically diversifies portfolio.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — highest portfolio-diversity upside; feel risk is decisive.**

### F9 — One-Room Convoy
1. **10-second verb:** flip/rotate one junction rule under time pressure to reroute autonomous units around a live threat/objective.
2. **Pleasure:** rapid triage and multi-unit near-misses in a comprehensible arena.
3. **Progression:** simple junctions -> unit roles -> temporary locks/hazards -> conflicting escort priorities and combo scoring.
4. **Dominant strategy:** establish a safe loop and only release units when clear.
5. **Boring play:** stall everything, micromanage one unit at a time.
6. **Muted trailer:** several tiny vehicles/creatures loop through one arena while junctions flip just before collisions/hazards.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if optimal play is pausing/stalling or if unit causality becomes unreadable above 3–4 agents.
8. **First comparison:** `GRIDROAD`/traffic routing visually, `Convoy` by title; final fiction/title must escape both.
9. **Content burden:** low if hazards/objectives/unit traits recombine on a few arenas.
10. **Accessibility:** adjustable speed/pause mode, strong route previews, shape/icon coding.
11. **Scope explosion:** autonomous pathing/reservations and edge-case deadlocks.
12. **Stories/clips:** strong near-miss/recovery moments.
13. **Theme stripped:** still a crisp real-time routing/triage game.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** moderate causal-system overlap, but crucially the player intervenes continuously rather than committing and watching.

**Verdict: SEMIFINALIST — compact and potentially sticky; must prevent safe-loop/stall dominance.**

### F10 — Heat Signature (working concept; title rejected)
1. **10-second verb:** paint/shift a thermal zone and observe guards/sensors/doors/agent path react in real time.
2. **Pleasure:** indirect manipulation and improvisational chain reactions.
3. **Progression:** binary thermal responses -> gradients -> competing sensors/crowds -> transient heat sources and timed infiltration.
4. **Dominant strategy:** create one permanent cold/heat corridor.
5. **Boring play:** precompute route, then paint zones and wait.
6. **Muted trailer:** heat/cold blooms alter cameras/doors/crowds, but visual language risks reading as generic stealth heatmap.
7. **Graybox kill:** kill if player spends more time waiting on AI than making decisions.
8. **First comparison:** famous `Heat Signature` by name; indirect-observation games such as `Sole Mistake` by interaction.
9. **Content burden:** medium due AI/sensor behaviors and stealth spaces.
10. **Accessibility:** texture/icon thermal layers, pause/slow options, non-color state cues.
11. **Scope explosion:** robust AI pathing, sensor state and emergent deadlocks.
12. **Stories/clips:** possible, but dependent on AI behaving legibly.
13. **Theme stripped:** indirect field manipulation remains interesting, but less immediate than F7/F9.
14. **Game #001 repetition:** moderate-high configure/environment -> autonomous agent consequence -> revise structure.

**Verdict: ELIMINATED — title collision is fixable, but AI dependence, indirect waiting and portfolio repetition collectively fail the semifinal cut.**

## 4. Semifinal slate

The tournament proceeds with six concepts:

1. **S1 — False Map Department** — best hook/system balance; immediate causality; moderate state-graph risk.
2. **S2 — Orbit Graffiti** — strongest action/portfolio diversification; existential movement-feel risk.
3. **S3 — Pocket Weather War** — systemic replay and visual battles; forecast/readability/balance risk.
4. **S4 — Echo Sculptor** — rare verb and low content burden; existential simplified-acoustics feel/accessibility risk.
5. **S5 — Creature Costume Inspector** — strongest character/comedy commercial surface; content cost and fixed-test-order risk.
6. **S6 — One-Room Convoy** — compact real-time triage; generic surface and safe-loop/stall risk.

Eliminated at Run 3:
- F4 Weather Locksmith — lost direct weather duel to F7.
- F5 Fish Compiler — tutorial burden + configure/release/observe portfolio repetition.
- F6 Impossible Elevator — weak market identity despite excellent scope.
- F10 Heat Signature — existing famous title, AI/indirect waiting burden, portfolio repetition.

## 6. NEXT TOURNAMENT ACTION

Run 4 should stress the six semifinalists as products rather than premises:

1. define an exact 5-minute first-session loop and an exact 30-minute mature-session loop for each;
2. define the smallest graybox and **binary kill thresholds** for each existential risk;
3. run dominant-strategy / boring-strategy attacks in more detail;
4. test whether each concept can generate at least 20 distinct challenge situations from a compact rule vocabulary rather than bespoke level gimmicks;
5. compare expected production burden for art, UI, simulation, content authoring and QA;
6. run another direct competitive check only where the refined loop changes the market comparison;
7. reduce six semifinalists to 2–3 finalists;
8. do **not** lock Product Thesis until the finalists have concrete session loops, scope ceilings and graybox falsification criteria.
